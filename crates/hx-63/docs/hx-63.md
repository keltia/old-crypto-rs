# Hagelin HX-63 — historical and engineering specification

**Project status:** Step 1 (research/specification only; no cipher implementation).

**Scope:** the full HX-63 electromechanical rotor cipher, not the earlier HX-61,
HR-61 or a reduced-feature HX-63 variant. This is an evidence ledger and a
requirements baseline, **not** a complete, verified algorithm. A configurable
simulation is not historical interoperability without independent vectors.

### HR-61 versus HX-63: documented facts and limits

| Feature | HR-61 ([C1], [C2]) | HX-63 ([P1], [S1]) | Implementation significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scrambler | Nine 41-connection permutation wheels | Nine 41-contact rotors, with an optional larger selection | Structural resemblance, not identical wiring |
| Alphabet/return allocation | 26 keyboard/printer lines, 15 connected return lines | 26 alphabet lines and 15 re-entry lines | Shared high-level partition; no verified pin-by-pin routing |
| Re-entry passes | Minimum one, **maximum sixteen** rotor-stack traversals stated for HR-61 | Re-entry documented; maximum needs HX-63-specific confirmation | Do not make sixteen a verified HX-63 bound |
| Modifier | A **single 41-contact-disk, 41-position modifier** described | Distinguishes 26-way input and 15-way re-entry modifications | Do not replace HX-63 typed modifiers with the HR-61 device |
| Stepping | **M-system** and adjustable pins | **M and MM** selected modes; configurable control chain | HR-61 provides no MM truth table or HX-63 event timing |
| Rotor wiring | Spring contacts may be rearranged; straight-through delivery arrangement described | Reconfigurable 41-contact wirings | Possible test fixture idea, **not** an authentic HX-63 vector |
| Positions | Motor-set starting positions visible through a window | Nine external rotor positions | Separate historical observation from shared API assumptions |

[C2, English pp. 3–4] explicitly reports an HR-61 M-system period of
`41^9` (approximately `1.63 × 10^15`); this is a **claim about the
HR-61's stepping period**, not a measured HX-63 period or a substitute for
verification of its M/MM controls. The translated text also describes the
fixed/spring contact construction and the customer's ability to rearrange
permutations. No rotor-wiring tables or corresponding plaintext/ciphertext
examples are supplied by [C1]/[C2].

[C3] lists both **HR-61** and **HX-62** separately as machines *in preparation
for manufacture* in October 1961; it provides no cipher equations or wiring.
[C4] helps place the HX-63 in the product lineage, but does not document its
internal encryption process. The [Crypto Cellar index][CC] describes the
HX research program's changing prototypes; this is development background,
not proof that a particular HX-55 or HR-61 mechanism survived unchanged.

### What this changes in the engineering plan

1. Model the common structural *possibility* of 41-contact permutations and
   a 26/15 channel split, but source HX-63 electrical routing from [P1]/[S1]
   or more specific documents, not from an HR-61 circuit inference.
2. Treat the HR-61's sixteen-pass statement as a **research hypothesis** for
   HX-63. A termination rule must be validated against an HX-63 signal path.
3. Keep the HX-63 input and reinjection modifier domains distinct; an HR-61
   41-position modifier does not justify merging them.
4. Do not use `41^9`, a mathematically plausible M stepping period, as a
   substitute for independently checked HX-63 per-character position traces.
5. Continue looking for **A-1149**, HX-63 connection/repair diagrams and
   complete keyed plaintext/ciphertext examples. None of [C1]–[C4] supplies
   an independent HX-63 known-answer vector.

## IEEE Spectrum restoration evidence (Step 1 supplement)

Jon D. Paul's [S3] is a **firsthand restoration account** of an HX-63 acquired
in 2020 for research associated with ARCSI. He also describes inspecting a
different French-held HX-63 in 2010. These are observations of physical
machines, not evidence that every HX-63 variant has identical internals.

### Observations and their engineering implications

| Observation in [S3] | Scope and implication |
| --- | --- |
| Nine removable rotors chosen from twelve, 41 contacts each, with adjustable stepping pins | Corroborates the rotor/contact model in [P1] and [S1]; provides no specific rotor permutation tables. |
| Nine release keys for setting initial positions | Corroborates an externally adjustable nine-position vector, distinct from internal wiring and pin selections. |
| A “modificator” with 41 switches each having 41 positions | Describes the *physical* adjustment assembly; it does **not** establish that the electrical transformation is an unrestricted 41-contact permutation. Compare [S1]'s 26-input and 15-reentry domains. |
| Signal makes forward and reverse rotor-stack traversals, with 15 additional reentry paths | Strong qualitative corroboration for reinjection. The narrative does not supply a numbered contact-path trace, termination rule or exact direction changes for a simulator. |
| Keyboard, motor/clutch, rotor movement and dual paper-tape printer execute a single mechanical cycle per keypress | Useful for understanding event boundaries; insufficient to determine whether the electrical mapping samples positions before or after stepping. |
| The restored unit produced printed output after repair | Establishes a potentially usable physical reference machine; does **not** supply reproducible known-answer data. |

Paul describes a four-mode control switch (off, clear/test, encrypt, decrypt).
Crypto Museum [S1] describes **five physical settings**: O, E, K, C and D,
where E is used to set the external key. Preserve the discrepancy pending
machine-variant or terminology evidence rather than normalizing these lists.

Paul reports approximately fifteen HX-63 units built; [S1] estimates roughly
50–200 from surviving serial numbers. These are **different published
estimates**, not a verified production count. Neither affects the cryptographic
algorithm.

### Research implications and possible external validation

The restoration suggests a route to **independent empirical tests**, subject
to access and documentation. A useful minimum experiment should record:

1. Specific machine and hardware/feature variant, plus rotor identities and
   ordered placement (without assuming the rotor wiring is publicly known).
2. Complete reproducible key: rotor wirings or a contact map; all pin patterns;
   modifier configuration; stepping mode and control order; starting positions.
3. Input mode and text normalization, plaintext, ciphertext, and if possible
   successive rotor-position observations and one or more contact-path traces.
4. Separate short tests for M and MM settings and for encipher/decipher, with
   reset conditions and the position-sampling convention stated explicitly.

**No such complete experiment appears in [S3].** The article's security or
key-space estimates must not be treated as cipher equations, validation
vectors or proof of interoperability. The author's description of the signal
flow is useful explanatory evidence, not an electrical schematic. Do not
infer a precise number of passes from its informal prose, nor transfer the
HR-61 sixteen-traversal bound to the HX-63 without confirmation.

Research leads: seek further restoration notes, photographs of the switch
and modifier wiring, any documented test output from Paul's machine, and
technical material held by ARCSI. None has yet been established as a
public reference implementation or independent HX-63 known-answer vector.

## Machine overview

The HX-63 was introduced in 1963 by Crypto AG. Its scrambler contains nine
interchangeable *Durchgangsräder* (D-Räder, pass-through permutation rotors),
with 41 configurable electrical passages and 41 adjustable stepping pins per
rotor [P1, p. 2; S1]. A larger set of twelve rotors was optional [P1, p. 4;
S1]. The rotor stack is electrically traversed using more paths than the 26
alphabet letters because 15 paths are reserved for **re-entry/reinjection**
[S1; P1, p. 3].

The original description distinguishes keyboard, operating-mode selector,
rotor converter and modifier, and twin-strip printer [P1, pp. 1–3]. Its
ciphertext normally consists of 26 letters; plaintext input supports other
keyboard symbols [P1, p. 3]. Optional PEH-61 tape equipment supports
five-level punched-tape traffic [P1, pp. 7–8]. Those physical I/O facilities
are out of scope for the initial cryptographic core.

## Key hierarchy

[P1, pp. 6–7] distinguishes **äussere Schlüssel** (external key) from
**innere Schlüssel** (internal key). External settings are those achievable
without opening the cover. The secondary [S1] additionally calls especially
infrequent physical rewiring a **basic key**; this three-tier naming is a
helpful engineering classification, **not** the two-tier terminology of [P1].

- **External:** initial position of each of the nine D-Räder for a message.
- **Internal:** modifier setting; selected M/MM method and control-chain
  arrangement; rotor physical order (and choice of rotor set); stepping pins;
  rotor internal passage permutation; optional outgoing-line/exit-disc
  permutations [P1, pp. 6–7].
- **Infrequent/basic (S1 terminology):** physical passage and exit-disc
  rewiring, which can require dismantling components.

[P1, p. 7] says that internal settings generally persist longer and the
external settings change often; it proposes potentially changing some
modifier/control-chain settings per message. **Do not hardcode** a universal
calendar cadence or treat example schedules as enforced machine behavior.

## Electrical permutation and reinjection — partial specification

**Documented:** the rotors provide configurable through-contact permutations;
the input-side modifier can rearrange connections to the converter, including
returns. Fifteen conductors from the output are fed back toward the input
[P1, pp. 3–4; S1]. The 26 alphabet and 15 feedback branches must retain their
physical roles in the model. [S1] shows a simplified cipher-mode signal-flow
block diagram and identifies a configurable exit disc.

**Engineering representation (derived, not yet authoritative):**

- A rotor passage map is a bijection on the contact index set `0..41`.
- Rotor identity, physical slot and stepping-control-chain position are
  separate concepts; permuting one must not silently permute the others.
- Input modifier and return modifier are typed permutations on their
  respective 26- and 15-element domains; any full 41-wire/exit mapping
  should be modeled explicitly rather than assumed identical to them.
- The traversal through the rotor stack and across re-entry channels must
  account for all connections and stop at an alphabet output rather than
  assume an ordinary 26-contact single-pass rotor cascade.

**Open:** exact contact indices, connection polarity, contact orientation,
entry/exit disk mapping, reinjection iteration and termination order, whether
all legal permutations yield a usable symbol mapping, and the exact reversal
for decrypt mode. Neither the patent nor a plausible cycle-walk algorithm
may substitute for checking the HX-63 circuit. No electrical algorithm is
approved for implementation at this stage.

## Stepping — what the original description actually says

[P1, pp. 4–5] specifies a configurable *Steuerkette* (control chain) of nine
rotors and two selectable modes. In both descriptions the **first wheel in
that chain always advances one step**. For a later wheel, inspect the active
pins (*Stifte in der Einflusslage*) of **preceding control-chain wheels**:

- **System M:** advance when **one or more** preceding wheels have an active
  pin in the influence position.
- **System MM:** advance when an **odd number** of preceding wheels have an
  active pin in the influence position.

For a *fixed snapshot* of pin activity, this suggests the following decision
layer, indexed by control-chain position `i` starting at zero:

```text
step[0] = true
step[i] = any(active[0..i])             // M, i > 0
step[i] = odd(count(active[0..i]))      // MM, i > 0
```

Here the right-hand slice **excludes** `i`. These equations are a **derived
logical restatement of [P1]**, not proof that the hardware samples all pins
simultaneously. The original text contrasts increasing stepping frequency
farther along the chain in M with approximately 50% for later wheels in MM
when active and inactive pin states are evenly distributed [P1, p. 5].

**Critical open stepping questions:**

1. Is the *Einflusslage* sensed before or after a particular rotor advances?
2. Are all pin values sampled from one pre-step snapshot, or is there a
   sequential carry/control-chain update within a character cycle?
3. Which physical pin offsets/rotation directions correspond to visible
   positions `01..41`, and do they differ between wheel locations?
4. Precisely when does stepping occur relative to the electric signal and
   mode selector? Are encryption/decryption timing rules identical?
5. Does the chosen control-chain permutation refer to wheel identities or
   mounting slots after rotor reordering?

**Gate:** locate A-1149, contemporary service diagrams, or an independently
observed stepping trace before claiming faithful dynamic stepping. Synthetic
truth-table tests of the equations above are useful but not historical vectors.

## Text and operator interface

[P1, pp. 2–3] documents an operating-mode selector including off, on/position
setting, plain, encipher and decipher. [S1] describes 26-letter cipher input,
and mode-dependent handling of shift and space with some letter substitutions.
The relationship between keyboard/teleprinter symbols, cipher-alphabet
characters and the printer must be specified separately. Do not import SG-41,
KL-7 or generic ITA-2 conventions without an HX-63-specific source.

## Verification strategy and completion gates

1. **Source traceability:** every machine-specific behavior tied to a page,
   diagram, photograph or independent observation.
2. **Static structure:** permutation bijections/inverses, valid rotor sets,
   pin lengths and distinct physical-vs-control orderings.
3. **Stepping:** M/MM truth tables first, then independently sourced
   per-character position traces (including carries, resets and wraparound).
4. **Electrical transformation:** reference contact paths through each branch,
   reinjection termination and encrypt/decrypt reciprocity against external
   evidence, not only self-inverse round trips.
5. **Historical test vector:** complete wiring/modifier/pin/order/position
   settings plus plaintext, ciphertext and preferably intermediate traces.
6. **Interop claim:** do **not** declare historical compatibility from
   synthetic keys, round-trip tests or the key-space size alone.
