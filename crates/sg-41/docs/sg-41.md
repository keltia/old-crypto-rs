# Schlüsselgerät 41 (SG-41): technical specification and validation plan

**Status:** Step 4 — typed wheel bank and trace-validated pin sensing; production stepping and encryption remain unimplemented.

**Scope:** 26-letter SG-41 (*Hitlermühle*), not the ten-digit SG-41Z.

## Machine and key model

The device is mechanical, without electrical rotors. Its six permanently installed pin-wheels have lengths **25, 25, 23, 23, 24, 24**, a total of **144 independently settable pins**. Each pin is inactive (`0`, left) or active (`1`, right). The complete set of pin bits forms the *internal/daily key*. The six wheel-window positions form the *external/message key*.

| Wheel | Length | Window markings | Cage contribution |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | 25 | `ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ` | 1 |
| 2 | 25 | `ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ` | 2 |
| 3 | 23 | `ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUVWX` | 4 |
| 4 | 23 | `ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUVWX` | 8 |
| 5 | 24 | `01`–`24` | 10 |
| 6 | 24 | `00 02 05 07 10 12 15 17 20 22 25 27 30 32 35 37 40 42 45 47 50 52 55 57` | inversion |

*Verify the alphabet lists above against Table 1 before using them as program constants.* In particular the physical alphabet omits J from wheel labels 1–4. The rotor/window coordinate and the pin-array index must not be conflated.

### Input/output alphabet and print head

- Keyboard has 26 letter keys; `J` serves as a **space** in the plaintext layer, and a literal plaintext J is conventionally written I.
- The machine prints plaintext and ciphertext on separate tapes; ciphertext is grouped by five.
- Four fixed scrambled letter rings are mounted on one print-head cylinder. The two outer rings are identical; the two inner rings are identical and in reverse order. The V/E control shifts the print-head alignment between encrypt/decrypt modes. **Do not implement this as a naive A–Z Caesar shift.**
- Table 2 (paper p. 12) is the authoritative 26-contact ring order. The two distinct ring sequences are transcribed in [the Step 2 fixtures](fixtures/sg41-reference.yaml) and checked for permutation integrity.
- Table 3 provides a local check: with PRN 8, encrypting `V` gives `I`; decrypting `I` gives `V` with the same state. This confirms direction and indexing, but is **not** a full implementation test.

## Cage / pseudo-random number

The SG-41 uses a **25-bar cage**, whose fixed lugs represent five weighted groups of bars: **1, 2, 4, 8, 10** for wheels 1–5. The sixth wheel participates in complementary/inversion selection. A bar can lock in three mechanical positions, but only its middle position engages the print-head gear. The net PRN is a number in `0..=25`.

At a conceptual level, for sensed wheel bits `b1..b6`, let `v = b1 + 2*b2 + 4*b3 + 8*b4 + 10*b5`. Wheel 6 toggles between direct selection and its complement, i.e. `v` or `25-v`, respectively. **Implementation must confirm polarity and timing using the exact cage diagrams (pp. 19–21) and published vector, rather than assuming this expression alone captures the complete machine.**

Importantly, the cage uses pins sampled **eight positions from the displayed windows**, after the first two stepping phases. Do not reuse M-209's lug/drum configuration or fixed once-per-character wheel stepping.

## Exact crank-cycle ordering

The 2023 paper's **Table 4, p. 16**, and explanatory notes on **p. 17**, define the chronology. Clock labels refer to the crank's orientation, not to wall-clock time:

| Crank interval | Action | State handling |
|---|---|---|
| Rest / 6–7 | Read and latch stepping pins at **−5** positions | Capture pre-step snapshot; key depressed |
| 7–10 | Print head aligns to entered character; paper advances | No cryptographic wheel mutation |
| 10–11: phase I | **Only if latched wheel 6 is 1**, each active latched pin of wheels 1–5 moves the wheel **to its right** one position | Apply target-wheel steps **in parallel**, not cascading |
| 11–12: phase II | If wheel 6's original latched pin is 1, **all six wheels advance once** | Uses original latch even if wheel 6 already moved |
| At 1 | Latch cage/PRN pins at **+8**, and freshly latch stepping pins at **−5** | Distinct snapshots; key released |
| Around 2–4 | Cage produces PRN and rotates print head | Uses the latched +8 pins; input is printed |
| 4–5: phase III | Every newly latched active pin on wheel *i* (1–5) steps wheel *i+1* once | **Parallel**; does *not* require wheel 6 to be active |
| At 5 | Output printed | Character complete for encryption |
| 5–6: phase IV | All six wheels advance one position | Final post-character step |
| At 6 | Crank locks | Final wheel state becomes next character's initial state |

**Essential stepping invariants:** wheel 1 does not step in phases I or III; phase II is conditional on the *original* latched wheel-6 bit; phase IV is unconditional. Phase III must use the **new** −5 snapshot, not the snapshot from phase I. Multiple step contributions to a wheel may accumulate across distinct phases. The paper explicitly warns that a wheel-6 movement caused by wheel 5 in phase I must **not** retrospectively alter phase II's decision.

The paper distinguishes the pin-sensing geometries at **−5 for stepping** and **+8 for PRN** (see pp. 11, 15–17). Implement positions with a clearly defined positive step direction and verify with published traces before interpreting offset arithmetic.

## Implementation boundaries and proposed next steps

This is a specification stage; **do not add a Cargo workspace member or crate skeleton yet**.

1. Transcribe and verify Table 5 bit arrays and Table 2 scrambled print rings; add immutable source-backed fixtures.
2. Define typed wheel positions/labels and stepping pin geometries. First test table-driven transitions using all 32 pre-character window tuples; record intermediate per-phase transitions where an independent simulator exposes them.
3. Implement phase I–IV as explicit stages, with separate latched snapshots, and make PRN timing observable to tests.
4. Implement the 25-bar/inversion mechanism with independent PRN fixtures; only then implement print-head encipher/decrypt mapping.
5. Add the canonical shared `old_crypto_rs::Block` façade and text layer when the stateful core is independently verified. Reuse core helpers as appropriate; do not duplicate the trait, grouping utilities, or common permutations.
6. Future integration: Divan benchmarks, demo and TUI, main README; no additions before known-answer tests pass.

Use `thiserror` in `src/error.rs` for any new errors; avoid incompatible public types and duplicating existing core abstractions. Cargo dependencies should use MAJOR.MINOR versions (except `old-crypto-rs` itself, which uses `version = "0"`), as established in the repository.

### Explicitly deferred

- SG-41Z numeric machine: separate design, not reproduced by changing an alphabet constant.
- Manual operating procedures beyond the cipher and conventional text layer.
- Unverified external vectors or guessed rotor stepping conventions.

## Step 3 typed foundations

`src/types.rs` defines checked `WheelId`, `WheelPosition`, `WindowLabel`,
`PinPattern`, and `PinSensor` types. The reference bit strings are interpreted
**only** in Table 5 row order; `PinSensor` records the documented −5 and +8
offsets but does not yet compute sensed bits. `WheelPosition::next_index()`
is a modular index operation, **not a claim about physical wheel stepping**.

The crate's unit tests validate all wheel labels (including the nonuniform
markings of wheel 6), wraparound, pin-string lengths and contents, the 144
source pin settings, and the sensor-offset distinction. Machine state traces,
step timing, and the cipher remain future work.

## Step 4 wheel-bank sensing geometry

`src/wheel_bank.rs` implements `WheelBank`, with checked physical wheel order,
visible window state, explicit one-position advances, and immutable pin
snapshots. Table 5 rows are indexed from zero in the increasing displayed
window order. At a visible index `i` on a wheel of length `N`, the mechanical
stepping pin is at `(i - 5) mod N`; the cage/displacement pin is at
`(i + 8) mod N`. Positive index movement is the direction of wheel advance.

This indexing convention reproduces **all 31 successive state transitions**
in Table 6 when Table 4's four phases are executed in the unit-test oracle.
The initial stepping snapshot is `010111` and the initial displacement
snapshot is `100010` (wheel order 1–6). Table 6 validates the stepping
sensor orientation; the +8 cage mapping follows the documented geometry but
will require independent ciphertext/PRN validation in a later step.

`advance_mask()` only applies a supplied set of wheel movements. The actual
production stepping decision layer and latch ordering remain Step 5 work.

## Step 5: production four-phase stepping

`src/stepping.rs` implements Table 4 and the four-stage timing diagram.
`step_before_cipher` latches the −5 stepping pins once, applies the
wheel-6-gated rightward steps (phase I), then optionally advances all wheels
(phase II), using the *original* wheel-6 latch for both decisions. Cipher
displacement pins (+8) must be read **after** these two phases.

`step_after_cipher` re-senses stepping pins for phase III (ungated rightward
steps) and then advances every wheel in phase IV. Both rightward-step phases
are simultaneous, rather than sequential cascades. The `PreCipherStep` and
`SteppingTrace` values expose each pin latch and wheel-selection mask.
`step_crank` is a wheel-only convenience wrapper and **does not encrypt**.
The 32 Table 6 pre-character window tuples remain the reference regression
test in `src/wheel_bank.rs`. Cipher validation remains a future step.

## Step 6: 25-bar cage displacement and sixth-wheel inversion

`src/displacement.rs` isolates the fixed cage calculation. Wheels 1–5
contribute weights `[1, 2, 4, 8, 10]`. The sixth wheel does **not** add a
weight: its active cage pin complements each of the first five cage pins
before accumulation, so that `steps = 25 - direct_sum` when active.

The API consumes an immutable `PinSnapshot` and returns `Displacement`
(the input snapshot, inversion flag, and a checked-by-construction `u8` in
`0..=25`). `sense_displacement` reads `PinSensor::Displacement` (+8) from
the live bank. It must be called **after phases I and II** and **before
phases III and IV**; sensing itself must not change wheel positions.

Figure 16's independent examples, `100110 → 19`, `100111 → 6`, and
`100100 → 9`, are unit tests. Inversion is also checked for every
possible five-bit input and the snapshot timing is covered using the
historical starting positions. These tests verify the cage logic, **not**
the complete Table 6 ciphertext, which additionally requires the physical
print-head ring transformations (implemented in Step 7).


## Step 7 — Historical print-head mapping

The four rings in Table 2 (paper p. 12) are stored as two immutable
26-letter permutations in `src/print_head.rs`. The outer rings (1, 4) share
`OUTER_RING`; the inner rings (2, 3) share `INNER_RING`. The sequences are
indexed from physical position 1 (the bottom of Table 2) toward position 26.

- **Encryption:** locate the plaintext on ring 4, advance by the displacement
  modulo 26, and read ring 2.
- **Decryption:** locate the ciphertext on ring 1, advance by the same
  displacement modulo 26, and read ring 3.

The source's Table 3 provides two independent direction checks:
`V` encrypted with displacement 8 is `I`, while `I` decrypted with
displacement 8 is `V`. Exhaustive tests check reciprocal conversion for
all 26 uppercase letters and all 26 cage displacements. The conversion
returns `None` on invalid characters or displacement values greater than 25.

This is only the character conversion layer. The integration of crank
stepping, cage displacement, print-head conversion, and message-level text
handling is reserved for subsequent stages.

## Step 8 — stateful character core

`Sg41Core` performs phases I and II, reads the displacement (+8) pins,
transforms the character using the selected print-head ring pair, then
performs phases III and IV. Invalid characters do not advance the wheels.
The 32-character historical known-answer vector is asserted for encryption
and decryption; the public `Block` facade is deferred.


## Step 9 — Validated machine configuration

`src/config.rs` introduces `Sg41Config` and validated construction of the
six-wheel machine from pin patterns and visible window labels. The builder
assembles a checked `Sg41Core` rather than allowing inconsistent wheel lengths
or positions to reach the character cycle. Configuration and wheel errors stay
in `src/error.rs` and use `thiserror`.

## Step 10 — Public SG-41 facade

`src/public.rs` exposes `SG41` using the canonical `old_crypto_rs::Block`
trait. Encryption and decryption restart from the configured initial machine
state on each call, and writes are limited to the shorter of the source and
destination lengths. The mechanical implementation remains in `Sg41Core`.

## Step 11 — Historical plaintext and ciphertext conventions

`src/text.rs` provides the SG-41 text preparation, display and grouping
functions: `prepare_plaintext`, `display_plaintext`, `format_ciphertext`, and
`parse_ciphertext`. The German historical representation of spaces and the
literal `J` is handled separately from the machine's letter transformation.
Unit tests cover valid and invalid text as well as grouped ciphertext.

## Step 12 — YAML key configuration and `sg41ctl`

The `sg41ctl` binary reads six-wheel configuration from YAML, parsed with
`noyalib`. Its original `encrypt` and `decrypt` commands support raw machine
letters and historical text mode. Input handling reuses the shared
`old_crypto_rs::helpers` CLI functions instead of maintaining a separate
stdin/positional-argument implementation.

## Step 13 — SG-41 benchmarks

Divan benchmarks cover SG-41 in the crate, and the root comparison benchmark
`b22_sg41` includes it alongside the other machine implementations. Benchmark
integration is separate from correctness assertions against the published
32-letter vector and full wheel-position trace.

## Step 14 — Demo and TUI integration

The root demo and `old-crypto-tui` expose SG-41 alongside the existing cipher
machines. The demo exercises the historical reference key, checks decryption,
and reports the 32-letter known-answer validation. This integration preserves
the shared machine interfaces and avoids duplicating the cipher core.

## Step 15 — Initial SG-41 implementation completed

The root README, references and integration points were reconciled. The typed
wheel model, stepping, displacement, print head, stateful core, public `Block`
facade, text rules, YAML CLI, benchmarks, demo and TUI all passed local tests.
The separate historical *traffic/message procedures* were not yet implemented;
these became the subject of Steps 16–22 below.

## Step 16 — Historical message-procedure specification

`docs/message-procedures.md` records the key hierarchy and operational framing
from Antal, Dahlke and Jahn's examination of original German SG-41 instructions.
It distinguishes the monthly pin table (`Monatstafel`), six-letter daily key
(`Tagesschlüssel`), disguise key (`Tarnschlüssel`), four-letter message
indicator (`Spruchschlüssel`), and the sender's quarterly station assignment.
The specification separates original documentary evidence from implementation
choices and from the still-missing independent complete-message ciphertext.

## Step 17 — Disguise-key types and indicator transformation

`src/message.rs` implements validated `DisguiseKey` and `MessageIndicator`
types and the pure indicator masking/unmasking operations. The four-letter
message indicator produces an eight-letter disguised value using the day's
scrambled alphabet and digit sequence, shifted according to the first three
*actual* ciphertext letters. Two additional caller-provided letters complete
the ten-letter transmitted header. All message errors are centralized in
`src/error.rs`.

## Step 18 — March 16, 1945 historical indicator known-answer

A historical YAML fixture records daily key `ACHUKW`, disguise alphabet
`SYIZBJEHOCKUGPDRVFTXLNMWAQ`, and digits
`96585138157834794289563262`, transcribed from the published table.
`tests/message_indicator_historical.rs` parses the fixture with `noyalib`
and verifies indicator `KNVP`, ciphertext prefix `CMQ`, shifts `57832629`,
disguised indicator `RIAVMDQO`, and complete header `RIAVMDQOWT`.
This is an independently sourced **indicator** vector, not a complete
historical SG-41 message ciphertext.

## Step 19 — Monthly/daily keys and station wheel positions

`src/procedure.rs` adds typed `DailyKey`, `MonthlyPinTable`, `StationNumbers`,
`Quarter`, and `StationAssignment`, plus `historical_config` for construction
of the machine configuration. The message indicator positions wheels 1–4;
the sender's station numbers position wheels 5–6 for both encryption and
decryption. The historical example has sender settings `18` and `37`.
As clarified by footnote 17 in the archival study, each monthly row contains
25 entries: wheels 1–2 use 25, wheels 3–4 use the first 23, and wheels 5–6
use the first 24. Station assignments change quarterly; the mapping is not
derived algorithmically from a date.

## Step 20 — Complete message transmission and reception

`src/transmission.rs` adds `encipher_message`, `decipher_message`,
`EncipheredMessage`, `DecipheredMessage`, and `MAX_MESSAGE_LETTERS`.
The message layer initializes the daily pin settings and all six initial
windows, encrypts the prepared text and caller-supplied padding, masks the
indicator from the first three ciphertext letters, and formats a ten-letter
header followed by five-letter groups. It enforces the implemented 600-letter
ciphertext limit. Decryption retains trailing padding because its original
length cannot be inferred from the transmitted text. `TransmissionError`
resides in `src/error.rs` and is re-exported there, not through a nested
`transmission::error` module.

## Step 21 — Historical transmission commands in `sg41ctl`

The CLI adds `message-encrypt` and `message-decrypt`, loading the monthly,
daily, station and disguise key material through YAML and `noyalib`.
The existing raw/text `encrypt` and `decrypt` commands remain available.
The CLI uses the repository-standard `Opts` naming and shared input helpers,
and delegates actual message processing to the Step 20 library API.

## Step 22 — Transmission regressions and verification audit

`tests/message_transmission_regression.rs` exercises a 600-letter boundary
message, over-limit rejection, damaged indicator detection, and incomplete
five-letter grouping. `docs/step22-verification.md` records the evidence
levels: a mechanical known-answer vector, a historical indicator known-answer,
and synthetic complete-transmission round trips are distinct kinds of tests.
`docs/message-procedures.md` was subsequently reconciled with the implemented
Steps 17–22. All stages passed local tests, and the top-level `justfile` was
updated to install `sg41ctl`.

**Remaining historical limitation:** no independently corroborated complete
wartime end-to-end message ciphertext vector is available. This does not
invalidate the mechanical and indicator tests, but it precludes claiming
independent full-traffic interoperability with a historical message.
